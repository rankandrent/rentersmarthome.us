## What renters should look for

**A renter's smart home is built from devices that plug in, screw in, stick on or clamp over existing hardware, so the apartment never changes.** Every purchase should pass three tests: it makes no permanent change, it works on a new network in a new apartment, and anything touching locks, thermostats, wiring or the building's exterior gets written landlord approval first. Our [complete smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) applies those rules step by step.

The usual order is plugs first, then bulbs, then the front door and climate. That keeps early purchases cheap and teaches you how your Wi-Fi and app handle more devices before you add a lock or camera.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**Platform compatibility outlasts any single feature.** Matter is the cross-platform standard backed by Amazon, Google, Apple and Samsung, and Thread is the low-power mesh network many Matter devices use. Choosing Matter devices means a move, a new roommate or a new phone doesn't force you to replace hardware. Our [Alexa vs Google Home comparison](/blog/alexa-vs-google-home/) helps with the assistant choice.

Other trade-offs to weigh across categories:

- **Hub or no hub:** Wi-Fi devices skip the hub but load your router; hub-based systems scale better.
- **Batteries or outlets:** battery devices go anywhere but need charging; plug-in devices need an outlet nearby.
- **Subscriptions:** cameras in particular may need a plan to save video, so check before buying.
- **Wi-Fi band:** many smart devices connect only on 2.4 GHz.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Most plug-in devices need no permission, but locks, thermostats, switches and anything outside your unit do.** Frame each request around reversibility: describe the device, explain that original parts are kept, and promise to reinstall them at move-out. Ask for the answer by email so you have a record.

Store originals in labeled bags (thumbturns, bulbs, thermostats, door viewers), photograph walls and fixtures when you move in, and use removable adhesive tested on a hidden spot first. When you hang anything heavier, our guide to [heavy-duty adhesive hooks for renters](/blog/best-heavy-duty-adhesive-hooks-for-renters/) covers damage-free options.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying in-wall switches, hardwired doorbells or full deadbolt replacements without approval.
- Mixing devices that don't share a platform.
- Putting high-draw appliances on standard smart plugs.
- Skipping the move-out routine: reinstall originals, peel adhesive slowly, factory-reset devices you leave, remove the old home from your apps and photograph every room.
