## What renters should look for

**Childproofing a rental relies on pressure, adhesive and clamp-on products that hold firmly but come off without holes.** Pressure-mounted gates, adhesive magnetic cabinet locks, outlet plugs, corner cushions and door knob covers handle most hazards without a drill. Our guide on [how to baby proof a rental without damage](/blog/how-to-baby-proof-a-rental-without-damage/) goes room by room.

Furniture anchoring is the exception that deserves a conversation. Tip-overs are a serious risk, and the most secure anchors screw into the wall. Ask your landlord about anchoring before you assume it's off limits; many landlords prefer a small, patchable hole to the risk.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**Every product in this category trades holding strength against easy removal, so match the product to the hazard.**

- **Gates:** pressure-mounted gates suit doorways and the bottom of stairs. Gate makers and safety groups say the top of stairs needs a hardware-mounted gate, because a pressure gate can push out. Check the width range, height and whether it has a walk-through door. Our roundup of the [best pressure mounted baby gate for renters](/blog/best-pressure-mounted-baby-gate-for-renters/) compares walk-through models.
- **Cabinet locks:** adhesive magnetic locks are invisible from outside and open with a magnetic key. The adhesive needs a clean, flat surface and curing time before you test it. Our guide to [magnetic cabinet locks for renters](/blog/best-magnetic-cabinet-locks-for-renters/) covers pack sizes and fit.
- **Outlet covers:** plug protectors are simple and renter-safe; covers that replace the outlet plate change the landlord's hardware.
- **Corner guards and knob covers:** adhesive corner cushions soften sharp edges, and knob covers keep small hands from opening doors.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Adhesive is the main deposit risk in this category, so test it first.** Stick one lock or cushion in a hidden spot, leave it for a few days and peel it to see whether paint or laminate lifts. Pressure gates can leave marks on door frames, so use wall protector cups or a small pad where the gate presses. If you anchor furniture with permission, note it in writing and agree whether you'll patch the holes at move-out.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Using a pressure gate at the top of the stairs.
- Mounting adhesive locks on textured, greasy or freshly painted surfaces.
- Testing a lock before the adhesive has cured.
- Leaving tall dressers and bookcases unanchored instead of asking the landlord.
- Pulling adhesive straight off at move-out. Peel slowly, warm stubborn pads with a hair dryer and remove residue gently.
