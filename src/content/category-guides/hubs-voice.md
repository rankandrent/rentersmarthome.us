## What renters should look for

**Pick the ecosystem first and the speaker second, because the assistant decides which plugs, bulbs, locks and cameras work together.** Amazon Alexa, Google Home and Apple Home all run on plug-in speakers and displays that need no installation, so the choice is about your phone, the devices you already own and how you like to control things. Our [Alexa vs Google Home comparison](/blog/alexa-vs-google-home/) walks through that decision for renters.

In a small apartment, size and sound matter. A compact speaker on a shelf or nightstand often does the job, and our roundup of the [best voice assistant speaker for small apartments](/blog/best-voice-assistant-speaker-for-small-apartments/) compares compact picks from each ecosystem.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**The most useful spec on a modern speaker is whether it doubles as a smart home hub.**

- **Matter controller:** a speaker that acts as a Matter controller can add Matter devices from other brands, which keeps your options open.
- **Thread border router:** some speakers and displays include one, which lets Thread locks, plugs and sensors connect without a separate hub.
- **Zigbee:** a few speakers include a Zigbee radio for older devices.
- **Screen or no screen:** a display adds camera feeds and a visual control panel; a plain speaker is smaller and simpler.
- **Phone fit:** Apple Home works best with an iPhone, while Alexa and Google Home work on both iPhone and Android.

Audio is the other trade-off. Small speakers handle voice and background music; if music matters, a larger speaker or a stereo pair is worth the shelf space.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Speakers and hubs never alter the property, so no approval is needed.** The renter-specific issues are placement and moving. Put the hub somewhere central with an outlet, away from thick walls that weaken wireless signals. Shared walls carry sound, so keep alarms, announcements and music at a level neighbors won't hear.

Plan for the move from day one. Choosing Matter devices means you can rebuild your setup on a new network without rebuying hardware. Our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) explains how Matter and Thread make a setup portable.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Mixing ecosystems without Matter, then finding devices that won't appear in your main app.
- Buying a speaker that can't act as a hub when you plan to add Thread devices.
- Placing the speaker in a corner where the microphones struggle to hear you.
- Forgetting privacy settings. Review voice recording history and mute the microphones when you want quiet.
- Leaving the old address in the app at move-out. Remove the home, reset devices you're giving away, and set up again at the new place.
