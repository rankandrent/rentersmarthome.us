## What renters should look for

**Smart lighting is the easiest place for a renter to start because bulbs screw into the fixtures you already have and come out again on move-out day.** Our roundup of the [best smart light bulbs for renters](/blog/best-smart-light-bulbs-for-renters/) covers the main bulb options, and our [Philips Hue vs WiZ comparison](/blog/philips-hue-vs-wiz/) explains the biggest choice in the category: a hub-based system or bulbs that join Wi-Fi directly.

The same no-tools idea extends beyond bulbs. LED strips add accent light under cabinets or behind a TV, and motorized shades or stick-on blind motors automate the windows.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**The connection type matters more than brightness or color.** The main options:

- **Wi-Fi bulbs** need no hub, which suits one or two lamps, but every bulb is another device on your router.
- **Zigbee or Thread bulbs** talk to a hub or border router and form their own mesh, which tends to stay stable as you add more lights.
- **Bluetooth bulbs** work from your phone nearby and suit a single room.
- **Matter support** lets bulbs show up in Apple Home, Google Home and Alexa, which helps when your next home runs a different assistant.

Also check the base and size (A19 for most lamps, other shapes for ceiling fixtures), whether you want white only or full color, and whether the fixture is enclosed, since some bulbs aren't rated for enclosed fixtures.

For strips, the adhesive matters as much as the LEDs. Our guide to [LED strip lights for renters](/blog/best-led-strip-lights-for-renters/) covers mounting that won't peel paint. For windows, our roundup of [no-drill motorized blinds](/blog/no-drill-motorized-blinds/) compares tension-mount shades with motors that clip onto existing blinds and curtains.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Bulbs, plug-in lamps and battery switches never need permission; replacing a wall switch or fixture does.** In-wall smart switches touch the building's wiring, so skip them unless your landlord agrees in writing. Battery-powered remotes and dimmers that stick on next to the existing switch give you the same convenience without rewiring.

Store the landlord's original bulbs in a labeled box, peel strip adhesive slowly (a little heat from a hair dryer helps), and keep any blinds you take down along with their brackets.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Turning off the wall switch. Smart bulbs lose power and go offline; tell roommates or add a stick-on switch cover or remote.
- Loading a basic router with dozens of Wi-Fi bulbs.
- Sticking strips straight onto fresh or flat paint without testing a hidden spot.
- Ordering tension-mount shades without measuring window depth at several points.
