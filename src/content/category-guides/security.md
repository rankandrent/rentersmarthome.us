## What renters should look for

**Renter-friendly security gear plugs in, runs on batteries or uses an existing opening such as the peephole, so nothing gets drilled into doors or walls.** Indoor plug-in cameras sit on a shelf, peephole cameras replace the door viewer, and adhesive-mounted sensors watch for leaks, smoke or open doors. Our guide to the [best security cameras for apartments](/blog/best-wireless-security-cameras-for-apartments/) is the place to start if you want a camera, and our [peephole camera roundup](/blog/best-peephole-camera-for-apartments/) covers the front door.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**Storage is the decision that shapes the long-term cost of any camera.** Some cameras record to a microSD card in the device, while others save clips only with a cloud subscription. Over a one- to three-year lease that difference adds up, which is why we compare plans in our [Blink vs Ring subscription breakdown](/blog/blink-vs-ring-subscription/) and our [Wyze vs Ring vs eufy comparison](/blog/wyze-vs-ring-vs-eufy-for-renters/). If you want a doorbell that saves video without a plan, see our [video doorbells without a subscription](/blog/best-video-doorbell-without-subscription-for-renters/).

Other specs to weigh:

- **Wi-Fi band:** many cameras and sensors connect only on 2.4 GHz, so make sure your router broadcasts it.
- **Night vision:** infrared footage is black and white; color night vision needs more light.
- **Privacy features:** a physical lens cover or a privacy mode helps when roommates share the space.
- **Hubs:** some leak sensors and cameras need a hub, which is one more device to place near an outlet.

Security in a rental also means safety. Smart smoke and CO alarms and leak sensors alert your phone when you're away, and our guides to [smart smoke detectors for apartments](/blog/best-smart-smoke-detector-for-apartments/) and [water leak sensors for renters](/blog/best-water-leak-sensor-for-renters/) explain which ones install without tools.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Indoor devices rarely need approval, but anything on the door's exterior or pointed at shared space should get a written yes first.** Leases often cover exterior fixtures, and buildings worry about hallway recording. Aim cameras at your own door, use motion zones to block neighbors' doors, and check local rules on audio recording, which can be stricter than video. Our guide to a [Ring doorbell in an apartment](/blog/ring-doorbell-in-apartment/) includes a short landlord message you can adapt.

Never remove or disable a landlord-installed smoke alarm; add your own device alongside it if you want phone alerts.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying a camera before checking whether saved video needs a subscription.
- Mounting with strong tape on painted trim without testing removal on a hidden spot.
- Recording common areas without asking.
- Leaving devices tied to your account at move-out. Remove them from the app and reset them before you pack.
