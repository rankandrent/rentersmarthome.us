## What renters should look for

**Start with the equipment you actually control.** If your apartment has a window, portable or mini-split AC with an infrared remote, a smart AC controller adds app control and schedules without touching wiring; our guide to the [best smart AC controller](/blog/smart-ac-controller/) explains how they copy the remote. If you have a wall thermostat on a central system, a smart thermostat may be possible with permission, and our guide to [installing a smart thermostat in an apartment](/blog/smart-thermostat-in-apartment/) walks through the checks. If the building controls the heat, focus on fans, curtains and plugs instead.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**Compatibility beats features in this category.** The specs that decide whether a device works at all:

- **Remote type:** AC controllers need an infrared remote. Wired wall controllers on some mini-splits won't work.
- **Thermostat voltage:** most smart thermostats are low-voltage (24V). Electric baseboard heaters run on line voltage (120V or 240V) and need a line-voltage model.
- **C-wire:** many smart thermostats want a common wire for steady power. Some models work without one, and others use an adapter installed at the furnace or air handler.
- **Wi-Fi band:** many controllers connect only on 2.4 GHz.

After compatibility, look at sensors and automations. Temperature and humidity sensors let a controller react to the room instead of a clock, while schedules and geofencing stop the system cooling or heating an empty apartment.

Not every fix is a gadget. Thermal curtains block afternoon sun, and a well-placed fan moves air through the unit at night. Our tips on [how to cool an apartment without AC](/blog/how-to-cool-apartment-without-ac/) cover those no-wiring options.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**A thermostat is part of the building's HVAC system, so ask in writing before you swap one.** Name the model, offer to keep the original and reinstall it, and suggest that maintenance handle the change. Line-voltage thermostats are dangerous to wire, so in a rental leave them to the landlord's electrician. AC controllers and smart plugs don't alter anything, though it's courteous to mention a controller on a unit shared with another apartment.

Before you disconnect a thermostat, turn off the breaker and photograph the wiring. Keep the photo, the old thermostat and its screws together for move-out day.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying a 24V thermostat for baseboard heat.
- Plugging a space heater or window AC into a smart plug that isn't rated for that load.
- Placing an AC controller out of the AC's line of sight.
- Losing the original remote or thermostat before move-out.
