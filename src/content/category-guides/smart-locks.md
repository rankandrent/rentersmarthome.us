## What renters should look for

**For most renters, the right smart lock is a retrofit model that replaces only the interior thumbturn and leaves the landlord's deadbolt, cylinder and keys in place.** From the hallway the door looks unchanged, the building's master key still works, and the original thumbturn goes back on in a few minutes at move-out. Our roundup of the [best smart locks for renters](/blog/best-smart-lock-for-renters/) compares retrofit and full-replacement designs side by side.

Before buying anything, check three things on your own door:

- **Deadbolt type:** retrofit locks are built for single-cylinder deadbolts with a thumbturn inside. Double-cylinder deadbolts (a key on both sides) usually rule them out.
- **Door alignment:** if the bolt drags or you have to push the door to lock it, a motor will struggle too. Ask maintenance to adjust the strike plate first.
- **Thumbturn clearance:** measure the space around the thumbturn so the lock body clears the trim and the door frame.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**The biggest trade-offs are connectivity, power and how you unlock.** Wi-Fi locks connect straight to your router but drain batteries faster; Bluetooth-only locks need a bridge or hub for remote access; Thread and Matter models work across Apple Home, Google Home and Alexa, which helps if your next apartment runs a different setup. Our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) explains how Matter and Thread fit together.

Unlock methods matter in daily life. Auto-unlock by phone location is convenient but depends on your phone's settings, a keypad lets guests in without an app, and fingerprint readers suit households that don't want to carry anything. Whatever you choose, keep a physical key on hand, because batteries do run out.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Anything that touches a lock deserves written landlord approval, even a retrofit model.** Many leases require that management can always enter in an emergency and that you don't change or rekey locks. A retrofit lock keeps the original cylinder, which is usually the point that wins approval. A short email that names the model, explains that the exterior and keys stay the same, and promises to reinstall the thumbturn at move-out covers most concerns.

Keep every original part (thumbturn, screws, mounting plate) in a labeled bag. On move-out day, reinstall them, test the deadbolt with the landlord's key, remove the lock from your app and factory-reset it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying a full deadbolt replacement without permission. It changes the keys the building relies on.
- Ignoring a sticky bolt. Motors fail early when they fight friction.
- Skipping the backup. Store a physical key somewhere you can reach if the battery or app fails.
- Forgetting the camera side of the door. A [peephole camera for your apartment](/blog/best-peephole-camera-for-apartments/) pairs naturally with a smart lock and also needs no drilling.
