## What renters should look for

**Hanging things in a rental comes down to matching the method to the weight: adhesive for light items, small-hole hangers for medium items and studs for anything heavy.** Removable adhesive hooks and strips leave no holes when used within their limits, and our roundup of [heavy-duty adhesive hooks for renters](/blog/best-heavy-duty-adhesive-hooks-for-renters/) covers the strongest damage-free options. For heavier shelves, mirrors and TVs, finding a stud matters more than any hook.

## Key specs and trade-offs

**The weight rating printed on the package is the number to respect, and it assumes a smooth, clean, painted surface.** Adhesive grips poorly on textured walls, wallpaper, fresh paint and damp bathrooms. Clean the spot with rubbing alcohol, press firmly and wait the curing time on the package before hanging anything.

When adhesive isn't enough, a stud finder tells you where you can anchor safely:

- **Magnetic stud finders** locate the screws or nails in studs, work through many wall types and need no batteries. Our guide to the [best magnetic stud finder](/blog/best-magnetic-stud-finder/) compares them.
- **Electronic scanners** read changes in wall density and can show the stud's width, but thick plaster can confuse them. Our roundup of the [best stud finder for thick walls](/blog/best-stud-finder-for-thick-walls/) covers models built for plaster and lath.
- **Metal and wire detection** on some scanners helps you avoid pipes and wiring before you drive any fastener.

Small-hole hangers are a middle ground. Many leases accept a few small nail holes as normal wear, while larger anchors usually count as damage.

## Landlord and deposit considerations

**Read your lease's wording on holes before you hang anything heavy.** Some leases allow small nail holes; others ban all fasteners. When in doubt, ask in writing, describe the item and the fastener, and offer to fill and touch up the holes at move-out. Photograph walls when you move in so you can show what was already there.

At move-out, remove adhesive hooks by pulling the release tab straight down along the wall rather than out toward you, which is how most removable strips are designed to release. Fill small holes with spackle, and ask the landlord for the paint color if touch-ups are needed.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Loading adhesive hooks beyond the rated weight, or using them on textured walls.
- Hanging immediately instead of waiting for the adhesive to cure.
- Trusting a single stud finder reading on plaster walls; confirm with a second pass or method.
- Drilling without checking for pipes and wiring.
- Peeling strips outward, which tears paint.
