Learning how to cool an apartment without AC comes down to three moves: keep the heat out, move the air at the right time, and stop making heat indoors. **To cool an apartment without AC, close blackout curtains on sunny windows during the day, open windows and run fans at night, and create a cross breeze with one fan blowing out and another window letting cool air in.** None of these tips needs installation or landlord approval.

These are the habits we'd use in any top-floor rental without air conditioning. If your unit does have a window or portable AC, a [smart AC controller](/blog/smart-ac-controller/) can make it far more efficient.

## Block the heat to cool an apartment without AC

**The cheapest way to cool an apartment without AC is to stop sunlight from heating it in the first place.** Windows facing the sun can turn a small apartment into an oven by late afternoon.

1. **Close blackout curtains** on sun-facing windows from mid-morning.
2. **Keep windows shut while it's hotter outside** than inside.
3. **Add reflective window film** if your lease allows it; many brands peel off cleanly.
4. **Shade the outside** of a balcony door with an outdoor blind or umbrella.

If you want motorized shades that close on a schedule, see our [no-drill motorized blinds guide](/blog/no-drill-motorized-blinds/).

## Move the air at the right time

**To cool an apartment without AC at night, open up at night and in the early morning, when outside air is cooler, then close everything before the day heats up.** This is the rhythm that keeps a rental cool without air conditioning.

5. **Create cross ventilation:** open windows on opposite sides of the apartment so air flows through.
6. **Use a box fan as an exhaust:** point a fan out of one window to push hot air out while another window draws cool air in.
7. **Face a fan inward at night** in the bedroom window to pull cool air over the bed.
8. **Set ceiling fans** to spin counterclockwise in summer so they push air down.

Here's a quick guide to fan placement:

| Situation | Fan placement | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two windows on opposite sides | One fan blowing out, other window open | Creates a through-draft |
| One window only | Fan in the window facing in at night | Pulls cooler air into the room |
| Hot top-floor unit | Fan blowing out of the highest window | Hot air rises and gets pushed out |
| Windows barely open | Air circulator inside the room | Moves air across your skin |

## Stop making heat indoors

**You can't cool an apartment without AC while appliances keep adding heat, and small apartments heat up fast from appliances, so shift cooking and laundry to the evening.**

9. **Skip the oven** on hot afternoons; use a microwave, air fryer or no-cook meals.
10. **Run the dryer and dishwasher at night.**
11. **Switch to LED bulbs**, which give off far less heat than old incandescents.
12. **Unplug chargers and electronics** you aren't using.

## Top-floor and small-window apartments

**Top-floor apartments and units with windows that barely open are the hardest to cool, so focus on shade and air movement inside the room.** Heat rises through a building, and a roof baking in the sun keeps the top unit warm well into the night.

What we'd do in a top-floor rental:

- **Close every curtain by mid-morning**, even on windows that don't get direct sun.
- **Run an air circulator across the room** when windows can't open far enough for a box fan.
- **Use the bathroom or kitchen exhaust fan** to pull warm air out in the evening.
- **Ask your landlord about the attic or roof**; some buildings can add ventilation or insulation.

For windows that only open a few inches, an air circulator inside the room beats a window fan, because it moves air across your skin, which is what makes you feel cooler.

## Humidity matters too

**Humid air feels hotter, so lowering humidity helps you cool an apartment without AC.** Run the bathroom fan during and after showers, dry laundry outside or at a laundromat on hot days, and keep lids on pots while cooking.

## Sleeping cool without AC

**Cool the bedroom in the evening, then keep the air moving across the bed all night.** Use cotton or linen sheets, put a bowl of ice in front of a fan for a quick blast of cooler air, and sleep in the lowest room if your apartment has more than one level.

## Check your lease before adding an AC

**If you decide fans aren't enough, check your lease before installing a window or portable AC.** Some buildings restrict window units for safety or appearance. ## Our verdict

**The best way to cool an apartment without AC is to block the sun by day, ventilate at night and cut indoor heat.** With blackout curtains, a box fan and good timing, most renters can make a hot apartment noticeably more comfortable without installing anything.
