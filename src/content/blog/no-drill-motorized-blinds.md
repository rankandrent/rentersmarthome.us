No-drill motorized blinds let renters automate their windows without putting a single screw into the window frame. **No-drill motorized blinds work in one of two ways: a tension-mounted motorized shade that replaces the blind, or a stick-on motor that automates the blinds or curtains already on your windows.** Both approaches leave the landlord's walls untouched, run on batteries, and leave with you on move-out day.

Below are the three no-drill options we recommend, how to choose between replacing and retrofitting, how to measure for a tension mount, and how to remove everything cleanly when your lease ends. For the bigger picture, see our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/).

## What are no-drill motorized blinds?

**No-drill motorized blinds are battery or solar-powered window coverings that install by tension, adhesive or clipping onto existing hardware instead of screws.** Traditional motorized shades mount with screwed brackets and sometimes wiring, which most leases treat as an alteration.

Renters have three renter-safe choices:

- **Tension-mount motorized shades:** spring-loaded brackets press against the inside of the window frame.
- **Stick-on blind motors:** a small motor attaches to the tilt wand of existing venetian blinds.
- **Curtain robots:** a motor rides the existing curtain rod or track.

**Retrofit the landlord's blinds when you only need light control; replace them with tension-mount shades when you want full blackout and lift automation.** Use this table to decide:

If you replace the landlord's blinds, store the originals and their brackets in a labeled bag so you can reinstall them before you move out.

## How to measure for a no-drill install

**Measure the inside width at three points and the frame depth before ordering any tension-mount shade.** A tension mount only works when the recess is deep, flat and square.

1. **Measure inside width** at the top, middle and bottom of the window frame, and use the narrowest number.
2. **Measure frame depth** from the glass (or window crank) to the front edge; compare it with the minimum depth on the shade's listing.
3. **Check the surface**: tension pads grip painted wood and drywall returns well, but tile or rounded corners may not hold.
4. **Look for obstructions** such as window cranks, alarm sensors or lock handles that sit inside the recess.

## Why renters shouldn't drill for motorized blinds

**Drilling into a window frame is one of the most common causes of security deposit deductions, and most leases treat new brackets as an alteration.** Screw holes in painted window returns need patching and repainting, and in many buildings the landlord's blinds are part of a uniform look from the street.

No drill motorized blinds avoid that problem entirely. Tension-mount shades press against the frame with spring pressure, so removing them leaves at most a faint mark where the brackets pressed. Stick-on motors and curtain robots never touch the frame at all, because they attach to the landlord's blinds or your own curtain rod.

If you ever want to compare the landlord's blinds with a motorized replacement, ask first. A short email works: explain that the new shades are tension-mounted, that no screws are involved, and that you'll reinstall the original blinds before you move out. Most landlords say yes to an upgrade that leaves no holes.

## Battery life, solar charging and noise

**All three picks are wire-free, so the practical questions are how often you charge them and how loud they are.** Battery-powered motors suit apartments because there is rarely an outlet next to a window.

- **Charging:** SwitchBot devices use rechargeable batteries, and an optional solar panel can keep a sunny window topped up. Graywind offers solar-powered motorized shades as well.
- **Noise:** small motors produce a short hum while moving. If a bedroom shade runs at sunrise, schedule it a few minutes before your alarm rather than at the same time.
- **Hubs:** SwitchBot's Blind Tilt and Curtain 3 add voice and away-from-home control through a SwitchBot hub, so budget for one if you don't already have it.

## Voice control and automations

**The SwitchBot picks work with Alexa and Google Home through a SwitchBot hub, and Graywind's listing shows its voice-assistant support, so the real benefit is automation rather than remote control.** Useful schedules for apartments include:

- Open the bedroom shades 15 minutes after sunrise as a gentle alarm.
- Close street-facing blinds at sunset for privacy.
- Close south-facing shades in the afternoon to keep a top-floor unit cooler.
- Open all curtains with one "good morning" routine on your [Alexa or Google Home speaker](/blog/alexa-vs-google-home/).

## Move-out day: removing no-drill blinds

**Removing no-drill motorized blinds takes minutes and leaves no holes.** Release the tension mount by turning the adjustment gear back, peel adhesive mounts slowly (warm stubborn strips with a hair dryer), and unhook curtain robots from the rod. Reinstall any original blinds you stored, then photograph each window for your deposit records.
