The best litter box for small apartment living is the one that fits your floor plan without shrinking your cat's bathroom. A litter box for small spaces has to do three jobs at once: take up as little floor as possible, keep litter and urine inside, and stop odor from filling a home where the kitchen, bed and couch share one room. We compared the boxes that the top-ranking guides recommend most often, checked every size against the maker or listing, and read what owners report after living with them.

Below are seven picks for studios and one-bedrooms, from a cheap corner pan to a self-cleaning robot, plus how to choose, where to put the box, and how to keep it from costing you part of your security deposit.

## Best litter box for small apartments: the short answer

**For most cats, the IRIS USA Large Top Entry Cat Litter Box is the best litter box for a small apartment: it is compact, fully enclosed and cheap.** If your cat is large, sprays high or is older, the right box changes, so match the box to the cat first and the room second.

Here is our quick version:

- **Best overall:** IRIS top-entry box, for small to average agile cats.
- **Best corner space saver:** Van Ness corner pan, for bathrooms and closet corners.
- **Best covered corner box:** Nature's Miracle hooded corner box, for big cats and high sprayers.
- **Best budget open pan:** Van Ness rectangular high-sided pan.
- **Best for less tracking:** Booda Clean Step dome, also good for kittens and seniors.
- **Best disguised box:** Good Pet Stuff hidden planter, for a box that sits in the living area.
- **Best automatic:** Litter-Robot 4, if you have the floor space and an outlet.

## The 7 best litter boxes for small apartments

**Every litter box here was recommended by at least two of the top-ranking small-apartment guides and is sold as its own Amazon listing.** We then checked specs against the maker or retailer and read owner reviews on Chewy, Walmart, Best Buy, PetSmart, Petco and the makers' own pages.

### IRIS USA Large Top Entry Cat Litter Box: best overall

A top-entry litter box is an enclosed tub your cat enters through a hole in the lid, so kicked litter and spray stay inside the walls. The [IRIS USA Large Top Entry Cat Litter Box](amzn:IRIS+USA+Large+Top+Entry+Cat+Litter+Box) measures 20.5 x 14.75 x 14.38 inches on its listing, with a 9.25-inch entry. That is one of the smallest footprints for a fully enclosed box, which is why Cats.com calls it an affordable option for small spaces and Hepper ranks it first.

The perforated lid doubles as a mat: litter falls off paws back into the box as the cat climbs out. IRIS includes a scoop that hooks onto the side. In Chewy reviews, owners repeatedly say far less litter ends up on the floor and that the lid is sturdy enough to keep dogs out.

The tradeoffs are size and agility. Cats.com notes the oval shape gives up some interior room and may not suit large cats, and one Chewy owner calls it a bit small for big cats. Hepper flags that it is not ideal for cats with mobility problems, so skip it for seniors. Owners also find the included scoop small.

### Van Ness Corner High Sides Cat Litter Pan: best corner space saver

Corner space is the floor most small apartments waste, and this triangular corner litter box is built for it. The [Van Ness Corner High Sides Cat Litter Pan](amzn:Van+Ness+Corner+High+Sides+Cat+Litter+Pan+Large) lists at about 18 x 18 inches, with two tall walls against the corner and a lower cut-out in front. The top-ranking small-apartment roundup names it the best space-saver, and The Vet Desk also lists it.

Chewy owners like how it tucks behind a toilet or into a closet corner and say the high back walls keep most litter in. The smooth plastic wipes clean, which helps in a bathroom you share with the box.

The triangle shape is also its limit. Cats.com points out that the litter bed is awkward for larger cats, and two Chewy owners say it is too small for an adult cat. Litter can still fly out through the entry cut-out. One Chewy owner reported the pan tipping forward when the cat stepped out, and Cats.com mentions similar customer reports, so keep it on a flat floor or a grippy mat.

### Nature's Miracle Advanced Hooded Corner Cat Litter Box: best covered corner box

Hooded boxes cover the litter with a lid, which hides waste and keeps spray off your walls. The [Nature's Miracle Advanced Hooded Corner Cat Litter Box](amzn:Nature%27s+Miracle+Advanced+Hooded+Corner+Cat+Litter+Box) combines that hood with a corner shape. Nature's Miracle lists it at 26 x 23 x 20 inches, with a charcoal filter rated for up to three months, antimicrobial plastic and snap latches. Catster and Hepper both include it in their small-apartment lists.

This is the corner pick for big cats. A PetSmart owner with a 14-pound male says there is plenty of room to turn around. Another PetSmart owner bought it for a cat that sprays high after other boxes failed. One reviewer on the Nature's Miracle site praises the high entry for keeping kicked litter in.

It is a big box, so measure first. On the maker's page, two owners mention latch trouble, one saying it tends to stick, and a few say replacement filters or lids were unavailable at times, so check that the listing you order shows the hood. One reviewer there reported leaks at the seam with a cat that pees standing up, while a PetSmart owner reported none.

### Van Ness High Sides Cat Litter Pan: best budget open pan

Sometimes the right answer for a narrow gap is a plain rectangle. The [Van Ness High Sides Cat Litter Pan](amzn:VanNessPets+HighSides+Cat+Litter+Pan+Large) in Large measures 17.5 x 15 x 8.5 inches on Chewy, with a 6-inch step-in at the front. Hepper calls it the best value, and The Vet Desk does too.

Cats.com's FAQ notes that behaviorists and veterinarians generally recommend uncovered boxes, because cats can see around them while they go. Chewy owners say the tall sides stop most kicked litter and that the pan fits a tight spot between a toilet and a wall. The smooth interior lets clumps slide out.

The catches are weight and size. One owner says the light pan gets tipped over, and a few others found the inside smaller than the outside numbers suggest. Two buyers also received a different color than they ordered, which matters only if the box sits in view.

### Petmate Booda Clean Step Cat Litter Box Dome: best for less tracking

Tracked litter is a bigger problem when your bed is ten feet from the box. The [Petmate Booda Clean Step Cat Litter Box Dome](amzn:Petmate+Booda+Clean+Step+Cat+Litter+Box+Dome) is a covered dome whose exit is a set of grooved stairs that wipe paws on the way out. The listing gives 22.5 x 22.5 x 19 inches and a charcoal filter in the dome. Catster and Hepper both recommend it, and Hepper calls it the best pick for kittens.

Owners on Chewy and Walmart say the stairs leave far less litter on the floor, the inside is roomy and easy to scoop, and the hood contains spray. A senior-cat owner on Chewy likes that there is no tall wall to climb over.

The most common complaint is the lid: many owners say it does not latch or sit tightly on the base. Several report the filter falling out or soaking up urine. A few say litter builds up on the steps themselves. It also takes real floor space, so it suits a one-bedroom better than a micro studio.

### Good Pet Stuff Original Hidden Litter Box Planter: best disguised box

## How to choose a litter box for small spaces

**Choose by your cat's size and mobility first, then by the spot you have, then by how much mess and odor you need to control.** A box that saves floor but that your cat avoids costs you far more in cleanup, so we always start with the cat.

### Footprint vs interior space

Cats.com's rule of thumb is a box at least as long as your cat from nose to tail tip and as wide as your cat is long. Outside dimensions overstate the usable litter bed, especially on corner, oval and automatic boxes. If your cat is large, lean toward the Nature's Miracle corner box or the Booda dome over the IRIS or the corner pan.

### Open, hooded or top entry

Each style trades privacy, mess and access, and we'd match it to your cat like this:

1. **Open pan:** easiest for kittens and seniors, best visibility for the cat, most litter scatter.
2. **Hooded or dome:** contains spray and hides waste, but traps odor inside if you scoop less.
3. **Top entry:** the least litter scatter and litter tracking, but needs an agile cat and clearance above.
4. **Planter or furniture:** best camouflage, but adds bulk and more parts to clean.

### Automatic boxes and outlets

Self-cleaning boxes save scooping and control odor, but they are tall, heavy and powered. Before you buy one, find the outlet, check that the cord reaches without crossing a walkway, and measure the height under any shelf. ## Where to put a litter box in a small apartment

**The best place for a litter box in a small apartment is quiet, low-traffic and away from food: usually a bathroom corner, hallway closet or laundry nook.** Litter-Robot's placement guide lists those spots and warns against putting the box next to food and water, next to loud or heat-emitting appliances, or in the bedroom or living room.

A few renter habits make small spaces work:

- **Bathroom corners** suit the corner pans and the Nature's Miracle hooded box; leave the bathroom door propped or add a cat flap only if your lease allows it.
- **Closets** work for open pans if there is airflow; never shut a cat out of its box.
- **Living areas** call for camouflage, which is where the hidden planter earns its spot.
- **Two boxes** are the common rule for one cat (one per cat plus one), per Litter-Robot's guide; a second compact pan can live in a closet.

## Protecting your rental and deposit

**Urine and scattered litter damage floors and baseboards, so protect the floor under any litter box from day one.** Landlords can deduct cleaning or repair costs from a security deposit, and many leases with a pet addendum spell out what counts as pet damage. Check your lease and local law for the details.

Here is what we'd do in any rental:

1. **Put a litter mat or waterproof tray under the box.** It catches tracked litter and any leak at a seam, which owners report on the hooded and planter boxes.
2. **Keep urine spray off the walls.** High sides, a hood or a top entry protects paint and baseboards in a corner.
3. **Scoop daily and wash the box monthly.** Odor that soaks into subfloor or carpet is the expensive kind.
4. **Photograph the floor before setup.** Date-stamped photos help at move-out.
5. **On move-out day,** lift the box and mat, clean the floor and baseboards with an enzyme cleaner, and check the corner walls for spray marks.

If you need to ask your landlord about a cat flap or an outlet for an automatic box, put it in writing. A short email works: "We'd like to add a self-closing cat flap to the bathroom door so the litter box can stay inside; we'll replace the door panel or restore it before we move out. Is that OK?"
