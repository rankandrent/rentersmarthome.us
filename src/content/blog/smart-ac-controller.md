An AC remote that lives on Wi-Fi is the easiest climate upgrade a renter can make. **The best smart AC controller for most renters is the Cielo Breez Max, because it works with window, portable and mini split units that have an infrared remote, and it needs no wiring or landlord approval.** If your apartment has a remote-controlled AC, you can make it smart in about ten minutes.

We like smart AC controllers because they solve a problem renters actually have: the thermostat belongs to the building, but the AC remote is in your hand. In this guide I'll explain how they work, how to check compatibility and which three we'd buy. If you can control your thermostat, read [can I install a smart thermostat in my apartment](/blog/smart-thermostat-in-apartment/) first.

## What a smart AC controller does

**This kind of controller learns your AC remote's infrared signals and sends them for you, from your phone, a schedule or a voice assistant.** Think of the controller as a Wi-Fi remote that never gets lost in the couch.

With one in place you can:

- Turn the AC on from your phone before you get home.
- Set weekday and weekend schedules.
- Use geofencing to switch it off when everyone leaves.
- Ask a voice assistant to change the temperature.

## Will it work with your AC?

**These devices work with almost any air conditioner that has an infrared remote, including window units, portable ACs and ductless mini splits.** Here's a quick compatibility check:

| Your AC | Works? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window AC with a remote | Yes | Most common renter setup |
| Portable AC with a remote | Yes | Keep the controller in line of sight |
| Ductless mini split | Yes | Check the remote is infrared, not wired |
| Window AC with dials only | No | Use a smart plug only if the AC restarts after power loss |
| Central air with a wall thermostat | No | That needs a smart thermostat |

## Setting it up in an apartment

**Place the controller where it can see the AC's infrared receiver, connect it to 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, then pick your AC model in the app.** Our setup checklist:

1. Find the receiver on your AC (usually a small dark window on the front panel).
2. Put the controller on a shelf or wall within line of sight, a few feet away.
3. Connect it to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network.
4. Select your AC brand and test on, off and temperature changes.
5. Add a schedule and turn on geofencing.

Use removable adhesive strips if you mount it on the wall, and peel them slowly at move-out. Our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) has more no-damage mounting tips.

## What to look for before you buy

**The right controller depends on your remote, your Wi-Fi and the smart-home app you already use.** These are the features we compare:

- **Remote type:** the controller copies an infrared remote, so a wired wall remote on a mini split won't work.
- **Sensors:** a temperature and humidity sensor lets the device react to the room, not just a clock. Sticky summers are where humidity triggers earn their keep.
- **Schedules and geofencing:** these do the most to lower your energy bill, because the AC stops cooling an empty apartment.
- **Voice and app support:** check the maker's current list for Alexa, Google Home and Apple HomeKit before you buy.
- **Wi-Fi band:** most controllers need 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, so make sure your router broadcasts it.

## Landlord-owned mini splits and shared units

**A controller is usually fine on a landlord-owned mini split, because it only sends the same infrared commands as the remote.** It doesn't open the unit, change settings in the wall or touch wiring. We'd still mention it to your landlord if the unit serves more than one apartment, and we'd keep the original remote in a drawer so it's there at move-out.

If your AC has no remote at all, a controller can't help. In that case look at a smart plug for a fan instead; our [Matter smart plug guide](/blog/best-matter-smart-plugs/) covers plugs that handle schedules well.

## Do you need a subscription?

**Core features like app control and an AC schedule work without a subscription on our picks, though some brands sell plans for extra features.** Check the current plan details on the maker's site before you buy, since they change over time.
