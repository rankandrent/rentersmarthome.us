## How a magnetic stud finder works

**A magnetic stud finder doesn't detect wood at all; it detects the metal screws and nails that hold the drywall to each stud.** Its magnet is pulled more strongly as it gets close to the fastener, and it sticks when it's right on top of one. That works the same way on a wood stud, where each drywall screw is the target, and on a steel stud, where the magnets feel both the screws and the metal framing.

Because drywall is screwed into studs in vertical rows, finding two or three fasteners in a line tells you where the stud runs. In most homes studs sit about 16 inches on center, so once you've found one, the next is usually close to that spacing. Fasteners aren't always perfectly centered, so we always confirm with more than one screw.

## Magnetic vs electronic stud finder

**Choose a magnetic stud finder for low cost and reliability, and an electronic stud finder if you need to see stud edges.** An electronic stud finder measures changes in the wall's density (capacitance), so it can map the full width of a stud, while a magnetic stud finder only shows fasteners.

Here's how the two types compare for apartment use:

- **Power:** magnetic models need no batteries; electronic models do.
- **What they find:** magnetic models find screws and nails; electronic models find the stud's density change and sometimes pipes or wires.
- **Walls:** thick or uneven walls can confuse electronic sensors; magnetic models struggle when plaster is too thick for the magnet to reach the nails.
- **Calibration:** magnetic models need none; electronic ones need to start over a hollow spot.

## How to find a stud with a magnetic stud finder

**Slide the magnetic stud finder slowly in a zigzag until it grabs a fastener, then confirm with a second fastener directly above or below.** It takes a minute or two on a typical wall.

1. Start near where you want to hang something, about switch height.
2. Move the stud finder slowly side to side, then up and down; magnets pull hardest right over a screw.
3. When it sticks, make a light pencil mark through the notch or beside the tool.
4. Find a second and third fastener in a vertical line to confirm the stud.
5. Check your height and level, then drill your pilot hole on that line.

## Renter tips: fewer holes, easy move-out

**For renters, a magnetic stud finder pays for itself by preventing extra test holes that you'd have to patch before move-out.** Every hole in a stud is a hole you meant to make, and fewer holes means less to argue about when the landlord checks your security deposit.

Before mounting anything heavy, I'd check the lease and send the landlord a short note: "I'd like to mount a shelf into the wall studs using two screws; I'll fill and touch up the holes when I move out.
