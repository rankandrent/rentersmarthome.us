## Installing a video doorbell without drilling

**Renters should use a battery doorbell with an adhesive or no-drill mount, and ask the landlord before putting anything on the door frame.** Our install order:

1. Read your lease for rules about door alterations and exterior devices.
2. Email your landlord with the model and a promise to remove it cleanly.
3. Charge the battery fully and set up the doorbell indoors first.
4. Clean the mounting spot with rubbing alcohol and let it dry.
5. Attach a no-drill mount made for your doorbell, or one rated for its weight, and let the adhesive cure before mounting.

Adhesive mounts work best on smooth, flat surfaces. On brick or rough stucco they may not hold, so you'd need landlord-approved screws or a different spot. If your door has a peephole, a peephole camera is an even easier option; see our [peephole camera guide](/blog/best-peephole-camera-for-apartments/) and our guide on [putting a Ring doorbell on an apartment](/blog/ring-doorbell-in-apartment/).

## How a video doorbell without subscription stores clips

**A video doorbell without subscription removes the monthly fee by keeping clips in local storage, which means you're responsible for keeping important clips safe.** Things to know before you rely on it:

- **Storage fills up:** older clips are overwritten, so download anything important.
- **Theft:** clips stored on the doorbell leave with a thief; storage on an indoor hub stays put.
- **microSD cards wear out:** replace them every year or two on a busy door.
- **Cloud is optional:** most brands sell plans if you later want off-site backup.

## Privacy in shared hallways

**A video doorbell in an apartment hallway should record your doorstep only. Set motion zones so your doorbell records your doorstep, not your neighbors' doors.** Lower the sensitivity in a busy corridor, tell neighbors across the hall what it covers, and check your local rules on recording audio.

## What you still get without a subscription

**Live view, motion alerts and night vision keep working on every doorbell in this guide, even without a paid plan.** That covers what most renters actually use: a phone notification when someone is at the door, a quick live look, and a recorded clip saved locally.

What you usually give up is off-site cloud backup and some advanced extras, such as long-term clip history in the cloud. Here's what we'd check in the app before buying:

- **Motion alerts:** can you set zones and schedules so a busy hallway doesn't flood your phone?
- **Night vision:** is the picture clear enough at night to recognize a face in a dim corridor?
- **Two-way talk:** can you speak to a courier through the app?
- **Package detection:** is it included locally, or only with a plan?

If any of these matter to you, read the manufacturer's current feature list, since brands change what's included over time.

## Taking it with you

**A battery doorbell moves to your next apartment in minutes: remove it from the mount, peel the adhesive slowly, and set it up on the new Wi-Fi.** Warm stubborn tape with a hair dryer and wipe residue with rubbing alcohol.
