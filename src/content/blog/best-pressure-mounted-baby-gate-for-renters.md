A pressure mounted baby gate is the renter's answer to childproofing: it wedges into a doorway with tension instead of screws. **Our top pick is the Regalo Easy Step Walk Thru Gate for doorways and the bottom of stairs, with the Summer Infant Extra Tall gate for climbers and the Cumbor Auto Close gate for busy parents.** One rule matters more than any product: a pressure gate never goes at the top of stairs.

We know childproofing a rental feels tricky, because most gate guides assume you can drill into the walls. In this guide I'll cover the gates we'd choose, where they're safe, and how to keep your walls clean for move-out. ## Never at the top of stairs

**A pressure mounted gate must never be used at the top of stairs, because a child pushing on it can knock it loose.** The top of the stairs needs hardware: a hardware-mounted gate screwed into wall studs or a banister.

Renters in a two-story unit or townhouse should:

1. Ask the landlord in writing for permission to install a hardware-mounted gate at the top.
2. Offer to fill and paint the small screw holes at move-out.
3. Use a pressure gate at the bottom of the stairs, where it is allowed.

If you're unsure, a banister adapter kit can reduce or avoid holes in the banister itself, but follow the gate maker's instructions.

## Protecting walls and door frames

**Wall cups, pads and correct tension stop pressure on the walls from marking paint, and keep a pressure gate from denting drywall.** Our habits:

- Use the wall cups that come with the gate, or soft pads made for gates.
- Tighten until the gate is firm, then stop; overtightening dents drywall.
- Check tension weekly, since gates loosen as they're used.
- Mount against the door frame trim when possible; it's sturdier than drywall.

## How to measure an opening

**Measure the opening at the height of the gate's pressure points, then buy a gate whose width range covers it, adding extensions if needed.** Measure top and bottom, because older apartments often have walls that aren't perfectly parallel. Check that the product is certified to current juvenile product standards, such as JPMA certification.

## Where to put gates in an apartment

**In most apartments, a toddler needs a gate in two or three places: the kitchen doorway, the hallway to the bathroom and the bottom of any stairs.** Walk your home on your knees and look for the openings that lead to hazards.

- **Kitchen doorway:** a gate in this doorway keeps a crawling toddler away from the stove and cleaning products.
- **Hallway:** a hallway gate closes off bathrooms and bedrooms at once.
- **Bottom of stairs:** a pressure gate is fine here and stops early climbing.
- **Balcony door:** a gate is not enough; keep sliding doors locked with a renter-friendly bar or lock.

Check your lease too. Most leases say nothing about gates, but if yours limits changes to walls or door frames, a pressure gate is the answer because it uses no hardware.
