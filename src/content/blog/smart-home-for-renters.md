A **smart home for renters** is built from devices that plug in, screw in, stick on or clamp over existing hardware, so the apartment itself never changes and your security deposit stays safe. Renters can automate lighting, locks, cameras and climate without drilling a single hole, and every device packs into a box when the lease ends.

This guide shows how to make your apartment a smart home step by step: what to buy first, which devices need landlord approval, how to choose an ecosystem that survives a move, and how to reverse everything on move-out day.

## The three rules of a renter smart home

Every device in a renter smart home should pass three tests before you buy it:

1. **No permanent changes.** The device plugs in, uses batteries, sticks on with removable adhesive, or swaps a part you can restore (like a thumbturn or bulb).
2. **Portable.** The device works on a new Wi-Fi network in a new apartment without a contractor.
3. **Lease-aware.** Anything that touches locks, thermostats, wiring or the building's exterior gets written landlord approval first.

These renter smart home rules quickly eliminate in-wall switches, hardwired doorbells and full deadbolt replacements, and they point you toward the categories below.

## Step 1: Start with smart plugs

**A smart plug is the easiest smart home device for renters because it needs only an outlet.** Plug a lamp, fan, coffee maker or holiday lights into a smart plug and you can schedule it, control it by voice, or switch it off from your phone.

The [Kasa Smart Plug Mini with Matter](amzn:Kasa+Smart+Plug+Mini+Matter+KP125M) is our favorite first smart plug: it's compact, supports Matter, and reports energy use so you can find power-hungry devices. Our roundup of the [best Matter smart plugs](/blog/best-matter-smart-plugs/) compares more options, including Thread models.

**Renter tip:** don't put space heaters or window ACs on a standard smart plug unless the plug is explicitly rated for that load. Use a heater with built-in smart controls instead.

## Step 2: Swap in smart bulbs

**Smart bulbs turn existing light fixtures into dimmable, color-tunable, schedulable lights.** Unscrew the landlord's bulbs, keep them in a labeled drawer, and screw in smart bulbs. When you move, swap them back.

The [Philips Hue starter kit](amzn:Philips+Hue+White+and+Color+Ambiance+Starter+Kit) is the most reliable smart lighting system thanks to the Hue Bridge, and Hue's battery-powered dimmer switches stick to the wall with adhesive. For a cheaper Wi-Fi option, look at Matter-compatible bulbs from brands like WiZ or Govee; our [Philips Hue vs WiZ comparison](/blog/philips-hue-vs-wiz/) explains the hub versus no-hub trade-off.

If your apartment's light switches control ceiling fixtures, a stick-on smart switch or the [SwitchBot Bot](amzn:SwitchBot+Bot), a small robot that physically presses the existing switch, keeps the wall wiring untouched.

## Step 3: Secure the front door

The front door is where renter-friendly smart home tech pays off most:

- **Smart lock:** a retrofit smart lock replaces only the interior thumbturn, so the landlord's key still works. See our [best smart locks for renters](/blog/best-smart-lock-for-renters/).
- **Door camera:** a peephole camera installs through your existing door viewer with no drilling. See the [best peephole cameras for apartments](/blog/best-peephole-camera-for-apartments/).
- **Entry sensors:** battery contact sensors stick onto doors and windows with adhesive and trigger alerts or lights.

## Step 4: Control heating and cooling

**Many rentals have window units, portable ACs or ductless mini-splits controlled by an infrared remote.** A [smart AC controller](/blog/smart-ac-controller/) like the [Sensibo Air](amzn:Sensibo+Air+smart+AC+controller) learns that remote and adds schedules, geofencing and voice control, without opening the air conditioner.

Replacing a wall thermostat is different: it touches HVAC wiring, so ask your landlord first, keep the original thermostat, and photograph the wiring before you disconnect anything.

## Choosing an ecosystem that moves with you

**The ecosystem decision matters more for renters than for homeowners because you'll reconnect everything after each move.**

| Ecosystem | Best if you… | Renter advantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon Alexa | Want cheap smart speakers and broad device support | Echo speakers double as hubs for some devices |
| Google Home | Use Android and want Nest cameras or displays | Nest hubs act as Thread border routers |
| Apple Home | Use iPhone and value privacy | Apple TV and HomePod run automations locally |

If you haven't picked one yet, our [Alexa vs Google Home comparison](/blog/alexa-vs-google-home/) walks through the choice. Whichever voice assistant you choose, prioritize **Matter** devices. Matter is the cross-platform smart home standard backed by Amazon, Google, Apple and Samsung, so a Matter smart plug or lock works with all of them. **Thread**, a low-power mesh network used by many Matter devices, makes battery sensors and locks faster and more reliable.

## What to ask your landlord (and what you can skip)

**Most renters never need to ask about smart plugs, bulbs, speakers or indoor cameras because those devices don't alter the property.** Ask in writing before you:

- Install any smart lock or keypad.
- Replace a thermostat or light switch.
- Mount a doorbell or camera outside your unit.
- Add anything to common areas.

Frame the request around reversibility: describe the device, explain that original parts are saved, and promise reinstallation at move-out.

## Your move-out checklist

1. Reinstall original bulbs, thumbturns, door viewers and thermostats.
2. Peel adhesive mounts slowly; warm stubborn pads with a hair dryer.
3. Factory-reset any device you're leaving behind for the next tenant.
4. Remove the old address from your smart home apps.
5. Photograph every door, wall and fixture for your deposit records.

## Start small, then build

**The best smart home for renters starts with one smart plug and a set of smart bulbs, then grows to a retrofit smart lock, a peephole camera and an AC controller.** Stick to Matter-compatible, no-drill devices and your apartment smart home will be just as smart in the next place you rent.
