faqs:
  - q: Can you put security cameras in your apartment?
    a: >-
      In general, a wireless security camera inside your own unit is fine for
      most renters, but read your lease first. Leases more often restrict
      devices on doors, balconies and common areas than cameras on a shelf, and
      local law varies, so check both.
  - q: Do wireless security cameras need a subscription?
    a: >-
      Not always. Cameras like the Wyze Cam v4, Tapo C210, eufy Indoor Cam C120
      and Reolink Argus 3 Pro record to a microSD card with no required plan.
      Others, like Ring and Google Nest, keep longer video history behind a paid
      plan.
  - q: Can you point a camera at your apartment door or hallway?
    a: >-

## How to choose an apartment security camera

**A wireless security camera for apartments is easiest to choose by deciding first on power and storage, then on mounting, video quality and which smart home app you use.** Those first two choices decide your monthly cost and where the camera can go.

### Plug-in, battery or solar: which apartment security camera fits

A plug-in camera is the easiest choice for most renters, because it never needs charging and it can record 24/7. Battery cameras, like the Reolink Argus 3 Pro, free you from outlets but usually record on motion only. Solar adds top-up charging for a balcony camera, like the eufy SoloCam S220, but only works where the panel gets light.

### No-drill mounting options (adhesive mount, magnetic base or stand)

A no-drill security camera usually just sits on a surface, which covers most indoor cameras in this guide. In a rental, we would try these spots first:

1. A bookshelf or media console facing the main entry.
2. The top of a fridge or tall cabinet for a wider view.
3. A stand or magnetic base, where the camera supports it.
4. A removable adhesive strip for a battery camera, tested on a hidden spot first.

### Local storage vs cloud

Local storage means a microSD card (local storage on the device) or built-in memory, so video stays with you and there is no monthly fee. A cloud subscription keeps clips on the maker's servers, which protects them if someone takes the camera, but usually costs a monthly subscription fee. Several picks here offer both, with cloud as an option.

### Video quality and night vision

Resolution in this group ranges from 1080p to 2.5K. In a small apartment, 1080p is enough to see who walked in, while 2K helps you read details across a larger room. Color night vision, offered on the Wyze Cam v4, Blink Mini 2 and Ring Indoor Cam, is easier to read than black and white.

### Smart home compatibility

Match the camera to your app. Alexa homes fit Blink, Google Home fits Nest, and Apple Home fits the Aqara G3 with HomeKit Secure Video. For a wider look at renter-friendly setups, our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) covers how these pieces fit together.

Live view and basic motion alerts are often free. Over three years, the math is simple even without exact prices. A local-storage camera is a one-time cost plus a microSD card. ## Can you put an apartment security camera in your rental?

**In general, yes: an apartment security camera inside your own unit is usually fine, but your lease can restrict devices on doors, balconies and common areas.** This is general information, not legal advice, so check your lease and local law.

### What to look for in your lease

Search your lease agreement for words like "alterations," "exterior," "common areas," "surveillance" and "attachments to doors." Those clauses usually matter more than any rule about a camera on a shelf. Damage clauses matter too, since screw holes and paint pulled off by adhesive can come out of your security deposit.

### How to ask your landlord

For anything outside your unit, like a balcony camera or a door-mounted device, we would ask in writing. A short email works: "Hi, we'd like to place a battery-powered camera on our balcony, facing only our own space. It needs no drilling or wiring and we'll remove it when we move out. Is that okay?" Keep the reply.

### Where cameras are usually fine and where they are not

Inside your living room, facing your own entry, is usually the safest spot. Pointing a camera out a window or through the door at a shared hallway is where trouble starts. For watching your door specifically, a [peephole camera for apartments](/blog/best-peephole-camera-for-apartments/) uses the existing peephole hole and sees only your doorway.

## Privacy rules for your apartment security camera

**An apartment security camera should stay aimed at your own space, treat audio more carefully than video, and tell the people who live with or visit you.** Privacy laws vary by state and city, so this is general guidance only.

### Common areas (hallway, lobby, laundry room)

Hallways, lobbies, laundry rooms and neighbors' doors are shared spaces where other people expect some privacy, and many leases restrict recording them. We recommend angling the camera so it sees your entry from the inside, not the corridor beyond it.

### Audio recording and consent

Audio recording consent is regulated more strictly than video in many states, and the rules differ. If you are unsure, turn off audio recording in the app and use two-way audio only for live talk. Remember that a lens cover, like Ring's, may not mute the microphone.

### Camera etiquette with roommates and guests

Tell roommates where each indoor wireless camera for renters sits, agree on rooms that stay camera-free, and use privacy mode or a shutter when everyone is home. A privacy shutter (privacy mode in the app, as on the Tapo C210) makes that easy.

## How to install an apartment security camera without drilling

**An apartment security camera can usually be set up in under an hour by placing it on a shelf, connecting to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and locking down the account.** Here is the order we would follow.

### Best no-drill placements

Put the apartment security camera on a shelf at chest height or higher, angled at your main entry, and keep the cable tucked along furniture rather than taped to the wall. For battery cameras, use a removable adhesive strip and press it on firmly so it holds.

### Wi-Fi and app setup

Many cameras, including the eufy Indoor Cam C120 and eufy SoloCam S220, connect on 2.4GHz Wi-Fi only. If you cannot change your router settings, check whether it broadcasts a 2.4 GHz network before you buy. Thick walls and crowded apartment Wi-Fi can weaken the signal, so test the live view from the exact spot before you settle on it.

### Securing your account

Your camera account needs a unique password and two-factor authentication turned on. A security camera account protects video of the inside of your home, so we treat it like a bank login. Update the app and camera firmware when prompted.

## Move-out checklist for an apartment security camera

**On move-out day, an apartment security camera should be unplugged, unmounted cleanly, wiped of your data and factory reset.** That way your deposit stays safe and the next tenant never sees your account.

1. Delete saved footage from the app and any cloud account.
2. Remove the camera from your account and factory reset it.
3. Pull the microSD card and keep it, or wipe it.
4. Peel adhesive strips slowly, straight down along the wall.
5. Check outlets and shelves for leftover cable clips.
