Can you install a smart thermostat in your apartment? **Often yes: if your landlord approves and your apartment has a compatible 24V heating and cooling system, you can swap in a smart thermostat and put the old one back when you move out.** The two things that stop most renters are the lease and the wiring, so we'll check both before you spend anything.

It's a common story: a renter buys a thermostat, opens the wall plate and finds two thin wires and no plan. This guide is the checklist we wish every renter had. If you're new to renter-friendly upgrades, our [smart home for renters guide](/blog/smart-home-for-renters/) is the best place to start.

## Can you install a smart thermostat in your apartment?

**You can install a smart thermostat in your apartment when three things line up: permission, a compatible system and the right power.** Here's the quick version:

| Your situation | Smart thermostat? | What to choose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central forced air or heat pump, C-wire present | Yes, with permission | Most 24V smart thermostats |
| Central 24V system, no C-wire | Usually, with permission | A model built for no C-wire, or one with an adapter |
| Electric baseboard heaters (120V/240V) | Only a line-voltage model | Mysa or another line-voltage thermostat |
| Building-controlled heat (boiler, no tenant thermostat) | No | Smart AC controller or smart plugs |
| Window or portable AC with a remote | No wiring needed | Smart AC controller |

## Step 1: Ask your landlord

**Ask your landlord in writing before you remove the old thermostat, because it's part of the building's HVAC system.** A short email works: name the model, say you'll keep the original and reinstall it, and offer to have maintenance do the swap.

Many landlords say yes, especially when you mention energy savings. A smart thermostat can also help them, since scheduling avoids heating an empty unit all day.

## Step 2: Check your heating system and wires

**Turn off the breaker for your HVAC system, pop the thermostat cover and photograph the wires before you buy anything.** That photo answers most compatibility questions.

Look for these signs:

- **Thin wires labeled R, W, Y, G and C:** a low-voltage 24V system, which most smart thermostats support.
- **No C-wire:** you'll need a model designed to work without one, or an adapter.
- **Thick wires with wire nuts:** likely line voltage (120V or 240V), common with baseboard heaters. A 24V smart thermostat cannot control these.
- **No thermostat at all:** heat may be controlled centrally by the building.

Then run the photo through the maker's online compatibility checker. Google and ecobee both offer one.

## The C-wire problem

**The C-wire (common wire) gives a smart thermostat steady power, and many older apartments don't have one.** Without it, some thermostats pull small amounts of power through the other wires, which can cause short cycling on certain systems.

You have two clean options. Some thermostats, like the Google Nest Thermostat, are designed to run without a C-wire in many systems. Others, like the ecobee Smart Thermostat Essential, support an adapter. ecobee's Power Extender Kit installs at the furnace or air handler control board, so you'll need access to the equipment, which is often in a locked closet in apartments.

## Smart thermostats that suit apartments

**For most apartments with central heat and air, start with the Google Nest Thermostat; choose ecobee if you need its adapter, and Mysa for baseboard heat.**

### Google Nest Thermostat: best for most apartments

For a typical apartment, the model we'd check first is the [Google Nest Thermostat](amzn:Google+Nest+Thermostat). Google designed it to work without a C-wire in many systems, and the Google Home app handles scheduling. Some systems still need a Nest Power Connector, so the compatibility checker matters.

### ecobee Smart Thermostat Essential: best if you have no C-wire

Missing a C-wire? The [ecobee Smart Thermostat Essential](amzn:ecobee+Smart+Thermostat+Essential) supports ecobee's Power Extender Kit, which adds the missing power at the furnace for most 24V systems. It's a good match if your building's maintenance team can reach the equipment.

### Mysa Smart Thermostat for Electric Baseboard Heaters: best for baseboard heat

Built for 120V and 240V heaters, the [Mysa Smart Thermostat for Electric Baseboard Heaters](amzn:Mysa+Smart+Thermostat+for+Electric+Baseboard+Heaters) is a line-voltage smart thermostat. Line-voltage wiring is dangerous, so in a rental we'd only let the landlord's electrician install it.

## When you can't change the thermostat

**If your heat is controlled by the building, you can still automate cooling and comfort without touching the wiring.** A [smart AC controller](/blog/smart-ac-controller/) adds app control and schedules to any window, portable or mini-split unit with a remote. For plug-in fans, our [Matter smart plug guide](/blog/best-matter-smart-plugs/) covers plugs that can run them on a schedule. Our tips on [how to cool an apartment without AC](/blog/how-to-cool-apartment-without-ac/) and the rest of our [heating and cooling guides](/category/climate/) cover options that never touch the wall.

## Move-out: put the old thermostat back

**Keep the original thermostat, its screws and your wiring photo together so you can restore it in ten minutes on move-out day.** We tape the photo inside the bag. Turn the breaker off, reconnect each wire to the matching letter, test heat and cool, then pack your smart thermostat for the next place.

## Our verdict

**Yes, you can install a smart thermostat in your apartment when your landlord agrees and your system is compatible; check the wiring first and keep the old unit.** If the answer is no, a smart AC controller gets you most of the comfort without any rewiring.
